Most trading gurus are selling courses, not trading. They run on demo accounts, show curated screenshots, and charge thousands for knowledge that isn't theirs. Here's how to tell the difference — and where legitimate education actually lives.

The Problem

The Industry Has a Fake Guru Problem

Trading education is a billion-dollar industry — and most of it is built on selling the dream of trading, not the reality of it. The people with the most visible presence are often the worst traders: they figured out that it's easier and more profitable to sell a course to aspiring traders than to actually trade the markets consistently.

"If their edge actually worked, why would they sell it for £500?"
The question worth asking before any purchase
5 Red Flags

Signs You're Looking at a Fake

These five things don't need context or nuance. If you see them — walk away.

01
Biggest Red Flag
They Only Show Demo Account Results
Demo trading has zero psychological pressure — no real consequences, no actual risk. Anyone can generate a $50K "profit" on a demo account. A real trader can show a live brokerage account with independently verifiable history. If everything you're shown is from a demo or a cropped screenshot with no account verification, it means nothing.
02
Red Flag
No Losses in Their History — Ever
Real trading has losing trades, drawdown periods, and rough weeks. Any mentor whose entire public record is winning trades — perfectly timed, never a loss shown — is curating a fiction. Real traders lose sometimes, and real mentors share that honestly because it's far more educational than a highlight reel.
03
Red Flag
The Course Is the Product — Not the Trading
When someone's primary business is selling a course, they are a marketer who talks about trading. Ask yourself: if this person had a genuine edge that consistently produced profits, why would they dilute it by selling it to thousands of people? Real trading mentors are typically selective and rare — not running mass-market sales funnels.
04
Red Flag
Lifestyle Comes Before Results
Lamborghinis, Rolex watches, Dubai skylines — before a single verified trade. The lifestyle content exists to trigger aspiration and lower your guard. Rental cars and borrowed apartments photograph the same as real wealth. A real trader's credibility is their track record. If the lifestyle came before the track record, the lifestyle probably isn't from trading.
05
Red Flag
Pressure Tactics and Artificial Urgency
Countdown timers. "Only 3 spots left." "Offer expires midnight." Legitimate education doesn't expire. These are conversion tactics from sales playbooks — they manufacture urgency to prevent you from thinking clearly. Good educators attract students through their results and reputation, not through manufactured scarcity.
⚠ The Single Best Test

Ask any mentor to show you their live funded brokerage statement — not a screenshot, not a demo account, not a prop firm account they bought yesterday. An independently verifiable live account with at least 6 months of consistent history. Most will have an excuse. Real traders don't need one.

Green Flags

What a Real Mentor Looks Like

These are the signals that tell you someone is actually worth learning from — before you spend a penny.

Verified live account history
Real funded account — personal brokerage or verified prop firm dashboard with months of consistent history, not just good weeks.
Shows losses honestly
Shares losing trades with analysis of what went wrong. The best educational content comes from failure, not success.
Specific, testable methodology
Can articulate exactly what they trade, their average R:R, and why they enter. Vague answers about "reading the market" are a warning sign.
Students with their own results
Real mentors produce traders who can show their own account history — not just testimonials. Proof in the community, not just the marketing page.

Before You Pay

The Pre-Purchase Checklist

Run through this before spending anything on trading education from any source:

Mentor Verification Checklist
✓
Ask for a live brokerage statement — not a screenshot, not a demo account, not a new prop account
✓
Check their history for losing trades — if there are none publicly visible, they're hiding something
✓
Ask what their average R:R is — a real trader can answer this immediately and precisely
✓
Find students with their own verifiable results — not just testimonials on the sales page
✓
Search their name on Reddit and trading forums — real community reputation is hard to fake
✓
If there's a deadline or countdown timer — that's a sales tactic, not a reason to buy. Walk away and revisit with a clear head.
Skip the Gurus. Start Trading.
Use a regulated broker to learn. Get funded through a reputable prop firm. Build a verifiable track record of your own.
